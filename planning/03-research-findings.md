## 1. Hands-free capture on iPhone (`research/capture.md`, gap 3, gap 9)

- `VNDocumentCameraViewController` (iOS 13+) exposes only `delegate` and `isSupported`, returns pages only after a Save tap, and has no per-page processing callback. Apple DTS said in January 2025 "there is no way to customize VNDocumentCameraViewController in that way" and confirmed in May 2026 that pages are returned only on Save. It cannot meet zero interaction or durability.
- `DetectDocumentSegmentationRequest` (iOS 18 Swift API; `VNDetectDocumentSegmentationRequest` since iOS 15) is the ML segmenter VisionKit itself uses: single document, non-rectangular pages, real time on Neural Engine devices (WWDC21). No Apple-published per-frame latency exists for it or any Vision request.
- iOS 26 adds `RecognizeDocumentsRequest` (paragraphs, lines, words, tables, lists, barcodes, plus data detectors for phone, email, address, dates, amounts and tracking numbers in 26 languages) and `DetectLensSmudgeRequest` (confidence 0 to 1, Apple's sample threshold 0.9, A14+, motion blur can false-positive). iOS 18 added an image-aesthetics "utility" flag that marks documents and receipts.
- iOS 17 responsive capture: `isResponsiveCaptureEnabled`, `isFastCapturePrioritizationEnabled`, `isZeroShutterLagEnabled` (automatic for apps linked on iOS 17+ on the highest-quality formats; excluded with flash, manual exposure or bracketing), `captureReadiness` and `AVCapturePhotoOutputReadinessCoordinator`. Responsive capture needs A12+, deferred processing iPhone 11 Pro+.
- Resolution facts from the SDK headers and WWDC26: 24 MP is "only serviced as 24MP via deferred photo delivery" (so with deferred delivery off it silently yields 12 MP, and deferred proxies can only be finalized through the Photos library); 48 MP is a single sensor frame whose processing "can take several seconds"; ZSL at 48 MP is undocumented. 12 MP gives about 345 to 367 ppi on a page that fills the frame.
- Apple's own sharpness sample uses variance of the Laplacian via vDSP on the luma plane at the 1280x720 preset because the kernel is noisy at full resolution. Apple DTS recommends a second lower-resolution video data output for Vision rather than analysing full-res frames.
- Commercial SDKs auto-capture with 1 to 3 s default hold times: Scanbot (Apryse-owned since July 2025) sensitivity 0.66 ≈ 2 s with a 200 ms inter-page delay; Dynamsoft requires 1 to 8 stable frames by IoU and area delta; the CameraX sample holds about 0.4 s. Pricing is quote-only except Dynamsoft Document Normalizer from $1,249 per year. Nutrient has no live capture at all. Android's ML Kit Document Scanner is a Play-services Intent UI with no camera control.
- The only on-device OCR measurements that exist come from the third-party `text_sight` plugin on an iPhone 16 (A18), one-shot on a 63-line page: `.fast` 101 ms, `.fast` with correction 199 ms, `.accurate` 404 ms, `.accurate` with correction 736 ms; live 1080x1920 runs give 19 frames per second fast and 5 accurate. Scaling by pixels (12 MP ≈ 5.9× 1080p) and silicon (A15 ≈ 15.8 TOPS vs A18 ≈ 35), full-resolution accurate OCR is estimated at 1 to 3 s on A18 and 2 to 6 s on A15, so it can never sit on the feedback path.
- Print-ready has a number: FADGI 2023 Modern Textual Records ≥ 294 ppi. At realistic 55 to 70% handheld framing, 12 MP gives 200 to 260 ppi, 24 MP 290 to 360, 48 MP 400 to 510. The lever is therefore the framing gate (quad ≥ 0.78 of the frame's long axis). Page size must be inferred from quad aspect, locale, text-line height, LiDAR on Pro models and the document class. The iOS SDK header documents `kCGPDFContextCreatePDFA` as "Output a PDF that conforms to PDF/A-2u" (iOS 14), which the output report missed; it is unvalidated and is a one-day veraPDF spike.
- Thermal policy has an authoritative skeleton (`AVCaptureSystemPressureLevel` serious means throttling is advised, critical highly advised, shutdown interrupts the session; a drop in max torch level is an early signal) but sustained 100 to 200 page throughput is unmeasured anywhere.

## 2. OCR and document-understanding models (`research/ocr.md`, gap 7, gap 8)

- No single model is both sub-second on a phone and archival-grade, so the stack must be tiered. Apple Vision is the only engine with measured sub-second iPhone latency; Apple Foundation Models accepts images only from iOS 27 and has no published latency.
- Claude 5.5 models transcribe text as well as or better than Gemini 3.8 Flash and Mistral OCR on text benchmarks (Opus 5.5 text 94.5 and Sonnet 93.3 on Datalab's OmniParseBench) but are weak at text localization (Haiku 5.5 at 32 to 46% on Roboflow's localization task) and return no word boxes. A full Haiku transcript costs about $0.0005 per page but 4 to 7 s of generation at a measured 167 to 235 tokens per second. Hence: Vision supplies geometry, boxes, dedup fingerprints and the PDF text layer; Claude supplies fields and line-indexed corrections.
- Camera photos cost every model 7 to 20 accuracy points (Wild-OmniDocBench), with end-to-end VLM OCR models degrading least. Top open OCR models (TeleOCR, OvisOCR2, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 Apache 2.0, GLM-OCR MIT) score 95 to 97 on OmniDocBench v1.6 and run 1 to 2 pages per second per A100 batched, 1 to 7 s single-page warm. Cloud OCR: Mistral OCR 4 $4 per 1,000 pages with boxes and confidence; Google, AWS and Azure plain OCR about $1.50 per 1,000. None of these is needed in v1 or v2.
- LLM post-correction of OCR: strong gains on noisy historical text (54 to 60% character-error reduction) but degradation and hallucinated entities on near-clean text (Boros et al. 2024, 14 LLMs, 8 benchmarks, pre-frontier models). Re-aligning corrected text to Vision boxes is cheap (banded character-level edit distance mapped 99.8 to 100% of words correctly in simulation) but cannot detect hallucination, so anchors use a two-of-three witness rule.
- Apple documents that `usesLanguageCorrection` corrupts codes ("C001" becomes "cool"), so the raw pass has it off; `customWords` can be seeded with known senders for the accurate pass.
- An instant provisional label is achievable in well under 300 ms after fast OCR on every iOS 26 device with rules, a per-user letterhead and sender memory (feature-print kNN over Claude-labelled pages) and a Create ML text classifier; text-only classification tops out near 90% on 16-class RVL-CDIP with clean OCR and about 75% with noisy OCR, so plan on 50 to 100 Claude labels per class before the classifier replaces rules. Apple's Foundation Models adapter toolkit is rejected (160 MB per OS model version, entitlement, 5,000+ samples, toolkit not iOS 27 compatible at the time of research).
- Foundation Models on device: about 3B parameters, 4,096-token context, Apple Intelligence devices only (A17 Pro and later), ~0.6 ms per prompt token plus ~30 tokens per second on iPhone 15 Pro (so 2 to 3 s for a full page), guided generation always runs the default guardrails. Image input, Vision's `OCRTool` and Private Cloud Compute are iOS 27 only.

## 3. Claude API pipeline (`research/claude.md`, gap 6)

- Models and prices (first-party, list): Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens up to 100K prompt tokens (5× above), cache reads $0.01, writes $0.125 (5 min) or $0.20 (1 h); Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10 with cache reads $0.10 per the live docs; Opus 5.5 $4 / $20. The 512-token minimum cacheable prefix applies to all three. Structured outputs (`output_config.format`, `json_schema`) are GA, stream, and are ZDR-eligible; incompatible with citations and prefill. Required properties stream first in schema order. First use of a schema pays a grammar-compile cost; grammars are cached 24 h.
- Vision: Haiku 5.5 is on the high-resolution tier (28-px patches, 2,576 px cap, 4,784 tokens max); a 1075x1520 image counts 2,145 tokens; `transformations: {"oversized_image": "error"}` makes the API reject rather than silently downscale, and `count_tokens` honours it, which is how to verify the tier for free. Above 20 images per request, images are capped at 3,000 px.
- PDF input: 1,500 to 3,000 tokens per page, no extra PDF fee, 128 MB request cap on the first-party API (the 32 MB figure is stale), 600 pages (100 when the context window is under 1M).
- Latency: Artificial Analysis shows Haiku 5.5 at effort low with reasoning at 167 tokens per second and 8.95 s time to first token, Sonnet 5.5 low at 105.7 tokens per second and 1.20 s TTFT; no public figure exists for Haiku 5.5 with thinking off, which is the configuration this plan uses. Thinking tokens are billed as output tokens, count toward `max_tokens`, and are reported in `usage.output_tokens_details.thinking_tokens` on the final `message_delta`.
- Refusals: HTTP 200 with `stop_reason: "refusal"` and a `stop_details.category`; Haiku 5.5 has no server-side fallback and no fallback credit; cyber and general-harms refusals are unbilled; the system card reports 0.17% over-refusal on the API. No server-side idempotency key exists. A spend-cap 429 carries no `retry-after`.
- Rate limits: input tokens per minute binds before requests per minute (about 913 pages per minute on the Start tier at ~2,200 uncached tokens per page); the $500 and $1,000 monthly caps bind at roughly 4,200 to 9,000 and 8,300 to 18,000 users at 150 pages per user per month. Cache reads do not count toward ITPM.
- Privacy: 30-day deletion by default, no training on API data, ZDR via sales (Batches and Files are marked not ZDR-eligible; Covered Models such as Fable and Mythos require 30-day retention), HIPAA readiness self-serve, `inference_geo: us` at 1.1× price.
- Cost per page at list, 1,100 px image + OCR text + 400 output tokens: $0.00044 on Haiku 5.5; image-only, text-only and larger-image arms range $0.00029 to $0.00080; Sonnet is 19 to 20× that. Embeddings: Voyage 4 lite $0.02 per million tokens with 200M free tokens per account; rejected for v1/v2 because Voyage trains on inputs by default.
- The hot-path measurement (p50/p95 TTFT, end-to-end, thinking tokens, refusal rate on 200 real pages, thinking-off vs adaptive-low, image vs text vs both, three image sizes) could not be run because no Anthropic key exists in the research sandbox. `tools/haiku_hot_path_bench.py` is ready to run with your key.

## 4. Duplicate detection and grouping (`research/dedup.md`, gap 1, gap 2)

- No shipping scanner app or SDK detects "already scanned"; it has to be built.
- A synthetic calibration run showed every image hash (dHash64, pHash64, wavelet, 256-bit DCT/PDQ proxy, 256-bit block-mean) places this-month-vs-last-month statements inside the same-page distance band (stand-mounted: same page dHash 0 to 4, sibling 1 to 5). A hash-gated skip would discard real pages.
- Text tiers (9-gram Jaccard over fast OCR) separate most siblings but not bills where only a date and an amount change; the Institutional Books 6-bit SimHash threshold collapses at 0.3 to 1% OCR noise. Only parsed key fields resolve the hard case, and OCR digit errors can flip those too, hence the "two agreeing anchors, none disagreeing" rule and the zero-false-skip release gate.
- Index scale is a non-issue: 200k 256-bit codes brute-force in 0.39 ms on one x86 core (measured) and occupy 12.8 MB; embeddings need int8 or binary quantization. MobileCLIP weights were re-licensed to Apple's research-only AMLR in February 2025, so the product uses Apple's FeaturePrint or an Apache-2.0 model.
- Page-stream segmentation: 0.93 to 0.95 page accuracy and 0.83 to 0.88 whole-stream accuracy on real enterprise data; no paper isolates the precision of individual signals ("Page N of M", letterhead continuity, sentence continuity, form-factor change, capture gap), so per-signal error rates must come from a labelled handheld corpus. Claude Haiku zero-shot pairwise boundary F1 is 0.87 to 0.97 on OCR text; vision input is no better and five-way multi-split prompts are measurably worse.
- Recommended: page = unit of capture and durability, document = unit of Claude inference; each open document is a growing prompt-cached Haiku conversation whose output includes the boundary verdict (about 5 calls and $0.003 per 5-page packet); split when uncertain, let Claude propose merges, auto-merge at ≥ 0.8.

## 5. Competitive landscape (`research/competitors.md`)

- About 60 products surveyed. No shipping product delivers hands-free, auto-advancing, multi-page capture with AI understanding and multi-destination user-owned storage. Closest: Adobe Scan's premium "High-speed scan" (countdown, auto-capture per page, tilt-pause feedback; iOS release notes in early 2026 let users try it free but saving needs a subscription) and a Google Drive Workspace Labs experiment on Android (September 2025) that auto-starts and auto-adds pages. Genius Scan's batch mode auto-detects and snaps. None chains documents without a Done tap, detects re-scans, understands the document, or verifies delivery to several destinations.
- Consolidation: Microsoft Lens retired with scanning disabled on 2026-03-09; Evernote Scannable unmaintained since December 2024 and paywalled at $49.99 per year.
- Understanding lives in silos: Paperless-ngx 3.0 (July 2026) has opt-in LLM suggestions and chat but only OpenAI-compatible or Ollama backends and Azure remote OCR; DEVONthink 4 integrates Claude but is Mac-centric; receipt tools (Veryfi, Dext, Shoeboxed, Expensify) understand only receipts.
- Multi-destination auto-export is paid (Genius Scan Ultra $39.99 per year) or absent. Per-page speed is undocumented by vendors; the only figures are a vendor-interested test (Adobe 2 to 4 s, Genius Scan 1 to 3 s, CamScanner 3 to 6 s with cloud) and hardware (ScanSnap iX1600 40 pages per minute, CZUR overhead scanners 1.5 s per capture with page-turn detection).
- The number-one complaint against Adobe Scan and CamScanner is forced accounts and cloud lock-in. Positioning the product as a front-end to the user's own storage, with Paperless-ngx as a destination rather than a competitor, attacks that directly.

## 6. Durable delivery to user-owned storage (`research/storage.md`, gap 4, gap 5)

- "Captured" must mean S1 local-durable: blob written with F_FULLFSYNC and atomic rename, ledger committed in SQLite WAL with `synchronous=FULL` and `fullfsync=ON`. Sub-second, survives crash, force-quit, reboot and 48 h airplane mode; does not survive app deletion or device loss. S2 "safe" (first verified off-device copy) is network-bound and system-scheduled and cannot honestly be promised sub-second without a service bucket.
- First off-device hop for v1: the iCloud Drive ubiquity container (OS-managed upload independent of the app process, visible in Files, survives app deletion, end-to-end encrypted under Advanced Data Protection). CloudKit `CKAsset` is rejected for page bytes (double quota use, invisible to the user, bound to iCloud Keychain); records are capped at 1 MB with assets exempt, fine for a ledger mirror.
- Google Drive: `drive.file` is a non-sensitive scope (no CASA assessment); full `drive` is restricted. Resumable uploads, `generateIds` for idempotency (409 on replay), `sha256Checksum` on `files.get` for verification, `appProperties` (124-byte cap) for id and hash, `indexableText` (128 KB) feeds Drive's own search, `changes.list` with non-expiring tokens for audit. A 401 `invalid_token` on a frozen bearer header is a benign refresh-probe-resume cycle.
- Synology: WebDAV Server over HTTPS (port 5006; the port numbers come from secondary sources because Synology's knowledge base is JavaScript-rendered and could not be fetched), File Station API `SYNO.FileStation.Upload` (RFC 1867 multipart) and `SYNO.FileStation.MD5` (asynchronous job) documented in the official guide updated for DSM 7.0; `SYNO.API.Auth` v6 supports `otp_code` and a device token for 2FA; sessions expire after 7 days by default. QuickConnect carries only Synology's own apps. App Transport Security cannot be loosened for self-signed certificates at runtime, so transport must be a DSM Let's Encrypt certificate, Tailscale with `tailscale cert`, or a user-installed CA profile. SMB cannot run on a background URLSession.
- iOS background execution: background `URLSession` with file-backed tasks enqueued while in the foreground (so they are not discretionary), reconciled with `getAllTasks()` on launch; `BGProcessingTask` for nightly audits; iOS 26 `BGContinuedProcessingTask` to drain a session after the user leaves. Data Protection classes are decided by lifecycle (Class C for anything a daemon touches, Class A for replicated vault blobs and the foreground-only content database).
- Do not build the file pipeline on PowerSync, ElectricSQL, Replicache or Ditto; write rclone-style connectors in Swift. The immutability-versus-rename contradiction is resolved by separating document identity (fixed id), version bytes (immutable from first commit) and label (name and path, changed by metadata-only operations). Deterministic PDF re-emission (fixed `/ID[0]`, `xmpMM:DocumentID`, `CreationDate`) makes replays byte-identical.
- A 30-page-per-minute burst needs about 4.8 Mbit/s if the first off-device artifact is a 1.2 MB JPEG or 1.2 Mbit/s for a 300 KB PDF page, so the compact artifact goes first.

## 7. Archival PDF output (`research/output.md`, gap 9)

- No Apple framework is documented to write PDF/A: CGPDFContext and PDFKit give Info-dictionary metadata and a PDF/X output-intent key but no XMP or `pdfaid`. The undocumented `kCGPDFContextCreatePDFA` flag ("conforms to PDF/A-2u" per the header) is a spike, not a plan. Commercial SDKs are quote-priced and only Genius Scan documents a PDF/A flag.
- Because a scanned-page PDF is structurally trivial (one image XObject plus one invisible glyphless text run per page), an in-house Swift PDF/A-2b/2u writer is the right call for a solo developer: JPEG pass-through, CCITT G4 or lossless JBIG2 for bilevel derivatives, glyphless invisible text with horizontal-scaling fitting (the Tesseract/OCRmyPDF technique), sRGB OutputIntent, XMP with a declared extension schema, trailer `/ID`, validated by veraPDF in CI. Multi-page assembly byte-copies the same objects so no re-encode can degrade the archive. OCRmyPDF and Ghostscript are server-side optional re-validators only.
- Enhancement chain on the GPU: perspective warp, Lanczos to 300 ppi, orientation, document enhancer and background division, white point, unsharp mask, grey-or-colour decision, JPEG at quality about 50. Dewarp (UVDoc: MIT, 8M parameters, MS-SSIM 0.785 on its benchmark, no published runtime) is v1.5.
- Capture settings that matter: exposure bias about +1 EV, `activeMaxExposureDuration` cap, `.near` focus restriction, smooth autofocus off, gate on `isAdjustingFocus`, geometric distortion correction on, white balance locked per batch, sRGB output. Orientation when pointing straight down: the rotation coordinator is gravity-relative and undocumented for the flat case, so use a phone-top-is-page-top prior, quad aspect, and a two-way fast-OCR confidence vote.

## 8. Search (`research/search.md`)

- v1 on-device: SQLite FTS5 (porter plus a trigram table, BM25 with column weights) via GRDB with bundled SQLite, a structured metadata table fed by one Claude extraction per document, on-device embeddings in sqlite-vec, reciprocal rank fusion, Core Spotlight donation for system-wide and iOS 18+ semantic search, `IndexedEntity` for Siri.
- Natural-language queries are parsed instantly by a deterministic grammar (NSDataDetector, synonyms, known senders; Apple Foundation Models `@Generable` as an offline refiner on capable devices) and refined asynchronously by a tiny Haiku structured-output call. Money questions are SQL aggregates over extracted fields; free-form questions use Claude citations over the top-20 chunks.
- The raw report embedded documents with Voyage and queries with an on-device model, which are incompatible vector spaces; the plan uses one on-device model for both.
- v2 service: Postgres with pgvector and FTS (or ParadeDB BM25) with tenant row-level security; Turbopuffer namespaces if vectors outgrow RAM; Meilisearch or Typesense only if their scoped-key UX is needed. Never use the Anthropic Files API as a document store.
- The archive stays searchable without the app: Google Drive indexes PDF text layers automatically (128 KB `indexableText` hint), Synology Universal Search indexes PDF content, Spotlight indexes the text layer on Mac.
- Evaluate with 150+ labelled queries, nDCG@10 and Recall@20 via `ranx`; grade extraction first because most search failures will be extraction failures.

## 9. Architecture and latency (`research/architecture.md`, gap 3)

- Native Swift 6 / SwiftUI on iOS 26 is the only sensible choice for a camera-heavy latency-critical app maintained by one person; cross-platform frameworks add a bridge between the camera and ML and a maintenance tax.
- The perceived-instant rule: draw a hard line at the durable local write; everything above it (quad detection, stability, dedup hint, write) is on-device with no network dependency; everything below it (OCR, label, Claude, PDF, upload) is progressive refinement into an already-visible card.
- Backend for the service phase: Cloudflare Workers with Queues and R2 (free egress) as the key-holding proxy and staging, Supabase Postgres and Auth with native Sign in with Apple; about $30 per month fixed, with AI cost dominating at every scale.
- No usable third-party time-to-first-token measurement exists for Haiku 5.5 with thinking off, and Apple publishes no Vision OCR latency, so a per-stage `OSSignposter` measurement harness is the first engineering task.

## 10. Privacy, security, legal (`research/privacy.md`, gap 10)

- The privacy architecture and the speed architecture are the same architecture: Vision OCR must run on-device anyway, so its word boxes can black out SSN, account, routing and medical-record regions in the copy sent to Claude at essentially zero extra latency.
- Three-class policy: Class A secrets exist in full only inside the archival PDF in the user's storage; Class B reference IDs flow in plaintext; Spotlight and Drive `indexableText` get summary, tags and Class B, never the raw OCR dump. Under the managed-tier posture (own org, ZDR, Haiku/Sonnet 5.5) Batches and the Files API are off-limits and Covered Models are excluded. BYO-key mode cannot promise ZDR; consent copy differs per mode while the App Store nutrition label is one superset.
- End users may use their own key provided calls go device-direct to `api.anthropic.com`, the key is Keychain-only, and the app never uses Claude.ai, Pro or Max credentials (consumer terms forbid sharing; the usage policy forbids routing through consumer subscriptions).
- App Store: guideline 5.1.1(ix) says apps handling sensitive data "should" be submitted by an organization; form the LLC and enrol as an Organization before submission because an individual-to-organization transfer changes the Team ID and forces a Keychain re-login. 5.1.2(i) requires consent before sending data to a third-party AI; account deletion in-app is required once accounts exist; `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption = false` holds while staying on OS crypto and TLS.
- Regulations: a consumer storing their own medical bills is not a HIPAA covered entity, but the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (amended July 2024) does cover non-HIPAA health apps that hold health data (individual notice within 60 days, FTC notice for any breach, penalties at least $53,088 per violation as of January 2025) and Washington's My Health My Data Act requires separate consent; both attach only when the developer holds the data, which v1 and v2 avoid. GDPR and CCPA/CPRA apply to a service. Hosted storage brings 18 USC 2258A reporting duties.
- Discarding originals: IRS Rev. Proc. 97-22 accepts electronic records once the system is tested and documented; Federal Rule of Evidence 1003 admits duplicates. Keep physical originals for vital records, wills, titles, notarized documents, bearer instruments and undeposited checks. EU launch (copie fiable in France, GoBD in Germany, qualified timestamps) is a separate workstream.
- Secure deletion: a Shred action must be honest about provider trash folders and NAS snapshots.

## 11. Business (`research/business.md`)

- AI cost is a rounding error: about $0.0004 per fully understood page on Haiku 5.5, so a 1,000-page-per-month user costs well under $1 in model fees; Sonnet on everything would lose money on heavy users at consumer prices. A Haiku-first pipeline with 10% Sonnet escalation keeps cost of goods under 10% of net revenue at $49.99 per year under Apple's 15% Small Business Program.
- Price anchors: Adobe Scan $9.99/month or $69.99/year, CamScanner $9.99/month or $59.99/year, Genius Scan Ultra $39.99/year; RevenueCat's 2026 median yearly price is $34.80. B2B capture is priced 10 to 100× higher per document (Dext $25.21/month for 250 documents, Shoeboxed $9 to $179/month, extraction add-ons $0.32 to $0.50 per document).
- Recommended model: free BYO-key local-first tier; managed tier at $49.99/year or $5.99/month with a 14-day trial, Family Sharing and a ~500-page fair-use cap; later a $99/year Pro tier (unlimited pages, email ingestion, tax-pack export) and per-client B2B seats for bookkeepers and property managers. Ship in the Business category; position on "zero taps, archived forever, searchable by meaning" rather than "PDF scanner" keywords; price on outcome, not AI, because model prices fell about 10× in a year.
- Risks: Apple shipping native scan-and-understand (Notes scanning and the free on-device Foundation Models already exist), model-price deflation eroding any AI premium, AI-app churn (41% more revenue per payer but 30% faster churn per RevenueCat), privacy expectations around tax and medical mail.
- Roadmap estimate for a solo senior iOS developer with AI assistance: v0 in 4 to 6 weeks, TestFlight +6 to 8, App Store +6 to 10, backend +3 to 4 months, 9 to 12 months total. The dominant engineering risk is the sub-second capture loop and the durability pipeline, not the model calls.

## 12. Physical-mail specifics (`research/mail.md`)

- Envelopes are cheap attachment pages (postmark date, return address, certified and Intelligent Mail barcode markers), promoted to a Claude call only for IRS, government, legal or certified mail. USPS Informed Delivery has no API; its daily digest email can be parsed through the same inbound-email address as e-bills.
- Form factors map to four capture modes driven by quad aspect and the first OCR pass: sheet, long strip for thermal receipts, card with glare compositing, booklet or photo. Faded thermal receipts and carbonless forms get on-device contrast normalization but the original crop always goes to Claude. Tri-folded letters that will not lie flat are a dewarp problem deferred to v1.5.
- Boundaries use physical-world signals desktop systems never have: the quad disappearing and reappearing, form-factor changes, the envelope sandwich, the flip gesture. Published page-stream segmentation reaches about 93%, so mistakes are assumed and per-page durability makes them harmless.
- A US personal-mail taxonomy with key fields per class, implied actions (pay by date, respond by date, keep for tax year, discard) and retention anchored to IRS rules (3 years normally, 6 if income under-reported by more than 25%, 7 for worthless securities, indefinitely with no return, property records until disposal plus the limitations period) is in `schemas/taxonomy.json`. Azure Document Intelligence's prebuilt tax models (W-2, W-4, 1095-A/C, 1098 family, 1040 and schedules, 22 1099 variants plus a combo model) are evidence that these fields are stable.
- Junk mail is tagged discardable and hidden by default but, per your requirement, never purged. Opt-out feeds (DMAchoice, PaperKarma) are an optional later feature.

## 13. Apple platform capability matrix (`research/apple.md`)

- Apple ships nearly the entire on-device pipeline: `DetectDocumentSegmentationRequest` (iOS 18), `RecognizeDocumentsRequest` (iOS 26, 26 languages, tables, lists, 10 data-detector types), `DetectLensSmudgeRequest` (iOS 26, A14+), `GenerateImageFeaturePrintRequest` (iOS 18 Swift API), Foundation Models (iOS 26, Apple Intelligence devices, text only until iOS 27).
- Camera: zero shutter lag, responsive capture, 48 MP formats, readiness coordinator (iOS 16 to 17); `LockedCameraCapture` and `CameraCaptureIntent` (iOS 18) and `AVCaptureEventInteraction` (iOS 17.2) give Action-button and Camera-Control launch, but the Lock Screen capture extension has no network, so it is deferred.
- Durability building blocks: iOS 26 `BGContinuedProcessingTask`, background `URLSession`, CloudKit `CKSyncEngine` for a ledger mirror.
- Search: GRDB with FTS5 (SwiftData and Core Data have no FTS), Core Spotlight `CSUserQuery` (lexical plus semantic on iOS 18+), App Intents `IndexedEntity`.
- Security: Data Protection classes, Keychain accessibility, App Attest and DeviceCheck for backend auth, Sign in with Apple, StoreKit 2.
- Device adoption: Apple measured 79% of iPhones on iOS 26 in June 2026; StatCounter US shows iOS 27 at about 15% in October 2026; iOS 26 and 27 support identical hardware (A13 and later), so an iOS 26 floor costs nothing in hardware.

## 14. Digital ingestion and actions (`research/ingest.md`)

- Every Gmail read scope is Restricted: 6-week review, annual CASA assessment, a 100-user cap while unverified, and a Limited Use clause that forbids training custom models on Gmail-API data. That single fact justifies a per-user unguessable forwarding address driven by user-side Gmail filters (zero OAuth) for v1 of ingestion, with Gmail OAuth deferred to post-revenue.
- Cloudflare Email Routing plus Email Workers is free with a 25 MiB message cap and a DMARC header, but `setReject` is a permanent rejection, not a retry signal, so durability must come from persisting the raw message to R2 before processing (or from Postmark inbound, which documents retries).
- On the phone: a Share and Action extension handing off through the App Group and a background URLSession; an iCloud Drive inbox watched with `NSMetadataQuery`; user-picked folders rescanned on launch; born-digital PDFs archived byte-for-byte with PDFKit text extraction and OCRmyPDF-style skip semantics for mixed files.
- Actions: EventKit with full Reminders access and write-only Calendar access, an app-side ledger of created items, a single passive daily digest, ZIP export packs with `index.csv` and `index.json` and a bookmarked combined PDF. Paperless-ngx mail rules and consumption folders are the prior art.
- All email and PDF text is untrusted input and is treated as data, never as instructions.
